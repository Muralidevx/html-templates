Two things bigger than the pathway itself

These came up walking through the gaps and aren't fixes to the YAML — they're questions about how this pathway gets triggered and consumed in practice.

Eligibility shouldn't depend on the note mentioning "cardiac"

Today, a pathway becomes eligible for a patient when their appointment note or problem list matches something the pathway is looking for. That works fine for knee — the note says "knee pain," the knee pathway fires. But pre-op cardiac isn't a condition-specific pathway; it's a check that applies before any non-cardiac surgery. If eligibility only fires on cardiac-sounding language in the note, a knee-replacement patient's note that never mentions the heart means this pathway may never even become eligible — regardless of what their actual cardiac history says.

Suggested fix: any time a surgical pathway (knee, hip, or future ones) becomes eligible, automatically make pre-op cardiac eligible too, rather than relying on the note to separately flag a cardiac concern. The two pathways then run in parallel and both recommendations surface together — this isn't a "which pathway wins" problem, it's a "make sure both get a chance to fire" problem.

The emergent-surgery question may not fit the current pipeline

If the system extracts guidance from the next day's scheduled appointments, that assumes lead time — a note already sitting there overnight for the LLM to read. A true emergency (patient rushed in, surgery same-day or immediate) may not have that lead time, or even a traditional "appointment" to run the pipeline against at all.

Worth raising with the team: the "is surgery emergent?" check may not be implementable inside a nightly-batch, appointment-based extraction flow as it stands — it may need a same-day or real-time trigger instead. Separately: where would an "emergent" flag even come from? Not claims (too lagged), probably not a structured CCD field — most likely the clinician's free-text procedure note ("urgent," "emergent," "STAT"), which the extraction agent would need to be built to catch.

Before this goes into the YAML

One item the new chart doesn't fully resolve — worth confirming with the clinical team.

Do implantable device and "other high-risk condition" still belong?

The old pathway's high-risk gate includes cardiac_implantable_device_present (e.g. a pacemaker) and a catch-all other_high_risk_cardiac_condition. Neither appears in the new chart's specific four-item early-trigger list (severe valvular disease, severe pulmonary hypertension, elevated-risk congenital heart disease, recent stroke). That's not necessarily wrong — it may mean a device alone isn't meant to trigger consult under the new guidance, or it may mean the source chart just didn't call it out explicitly. Confirm with the team whether to drop these, keep them as-is, or fold them into one of the four named triggers.
